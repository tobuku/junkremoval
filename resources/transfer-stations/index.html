What Are Transfer Stations?

Transfer stations and convenience centers are county-operated facilities where Hawaii residents can drop off household waste, bulky items, green waste, and recyclables. These sites serve as intermediate collection points — waste is consolidated here before being transported to a landfill or processing facility. For residents who miss curbside pickup, need to dispose of oversized items, or simply have more waste than their regular collection can handle, transfer stations are the most affordable and accessible option.

Each Hawaiian county operates its own network of stations with slightly different rules and accepted materials. In general, most transfer stations across all islands accept the following:

  • Household trash — bagged residential garbage, general clutter
  • Bulky items — furniture, mattresses, large appliances (refrigerators, washers, dryers)
  • Green waste — yard trimmings, palm fronds, tree branches, grass clippings
  • Recyclables — cardboard, glass bottles, aluminum cans, plastics #1 and #2
  • Scrap metal — accepted at select locations; check before hauling

Hazardous materials — including paint, solvents, pesticides, propane tanks, and automotive batteries — are not accepted at transfer stations. Each county holds periodic household hazardous waste collection events for these items. Visit your county's environmental services website for upcoming dates.

Residential self-haulers on Oahu, Kauai, and Hawaii Island can use transfer stations at no charge with a valid Hawaii ID. Maui County charges by weight at the Central Maui Landfill. Commercial haulers pay tipping fees at all locations.

Common Questions

Transfer Station FAQ

Most Hawaii transfer stations accept household trash, bulky items (furniture, mattresses, appliances), green waste (yard trimmings, palm fronds), and recyclables (cardboard, glass, aluminum, plastics). Some stations also accept scrap metal, tires, and used motor oil. Hazardous materials such as paint, pesticides, propane tanks, and batteries are generally not accepted and must go through county-sponsored hazmat collection events.
Fees vary by county. On Oahu, the City and County of Honolulu transfer stations and convenience centers are free for residential self-haulers with a valid Hawaii ID. Maui County charges fees at the Central Maui Landfill based on weight. Hawaii County transfer stations are generally free for household quantities. Kauai County's Lihue Transfer Station accepts residential waste at no charge. Commercial haulers are charged tipping fees at all locations.
Hours vary by location and island. Oahu convenience centers typically operate 7 AM to 6 PM daily, while transfer stations run Monday through Friday 10 AM to 6 PM and Saturdays 7 AM to 6 PM. Hawaii Island stations generally run 6 AM to 6 PM Monday through Saturday. Maui's Central Maui Landfill operates 6 AM to 3 PM Monday through Saturday. Always check individual station hours before visiting, as holiday schedules may differ.
No appointment is needed for residential drop-offs at any Hawaii transfer station or convenience center. Simply drive up during operating hours with your load. Some locations may require a valid Hawaii driver's license or state ID to confirm residency. During peak times — especially Saturday mornings — expect wait times of 15 to 30 minutes at popular stations like Kapaa and Keehi on Oahu.
Transfer stations in Hawaii generally do not accept hazardous waste (paint, solvents, pesticides, pool chemicals), medical waste, asbestos-containing materials, explosives or ammunition, radioactive materials, or large quantities of liquid waste. Some stations also refuse tires, car batteries, and e-waste — these items often have dedicated drop-off programs through your county. Check with your local county environmental services office for proper disposal of restricted items.
