Junk Removal in Mountain View, Hawaii Island

This area includes Mountain View Town, Fern Acres, Kulani, and Glenwood — a mix of older plantation-era homes, off-grid homesteads, small farms, and newer residential lots carved from dense rainforest. Mountain View Junk Removal & Hauling Services

Mountain View Town itself is a small cluster of homes, a few shops, and community facilities along the old Volcano Highway. But the surrounding area — Fern Acres, Kulani, and the parcels along the roads branching off Highway 11 — holds a growing population of residents on one-to-five-acre lots. Many of these properties were originally agricultural land and still carry the accumulated material of decades: old farming equipment, broken vehicles, rotted lumber, rusted metal roofing, and household goods stored in sheds, containers, and makeshift structures. Professional junk removal is often the only practical way to clear these lots, especially when the volume exceeds what a pickup truck can handle.

The Upper Puna climate — cool, wet, and overcast much of the year — accelerates the deterioration of stored goods. Furniture left in unenclosed carports develops mold. Metal tools and appliances rust quickly. Lumber rots. When a property changes hands or an owner finally decides to clear out years of accumulation, the result is often a large, heavy, moisture-damaged load that requires a proper hauling truck and crew. Junk removal providers experienced with Mountain View and Fern Acres know to expect these conditions and come prepared with the right equipment.

Glenwood, located between Mountain View and Volcano along Highway 11, is even more rural. Properties here are often larger, more isolated, and accessed by unpaved roads through dense ohia forest. Estate cleanouts, lot clearing, and construction debris removal are common requests from Glenwood property owners. The area also sees steady building activity as new owners develop previously vacant land, generating drywall, lumber, roofing, and concrete waste that needs professional hauling.

Mountain View's proximity to Hilo — about 18 miles down Highway 11 — keeps junk removal costs more reasonable than in communities farther from disposal facilities. Most junk is transported to the East Hawaii Transfer Station in Hilo. Recyclable metals, electronics, and appliances are diverted to recycling facilities. Green waste from Mountain View's lush environment is a regular hauling request, particularly after storms bring down trees and scatter branches across properties.
